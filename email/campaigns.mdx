---
title: "Build and send an email campaign"
description: "Create, test, send, or schedule an email campaign and confirm its progress."
---

A campaign sends one email to an email list or a dynamic segment. Open **Marketing → Email Campaigns** and choose **New Campaign**.

## Before you begin

You need permission to create or edit campaigns. Sending requires separate permission; a Manager can prepare a campaign but cannot send it under the standard roles.

Prepare a [verified sending domain](/settings/sending-domains), a sender address on that domain, and an [email list or segment](/email/lists-and-segments). Include a working unsubscribe link in the message. Advocora sends campaigns natively; an external email-provider integration is optional.

## Build the campaign

1. In **Basic Details**, enter the internal campaign name, **Subject Line**, optional **Preheader Text**, sender name/email, and optional reply-to address. Review tracking settings.
2. In **Design Email**, choose a template or build the message. Add your content, links, and unsubscribe merge tag. Check any personalization fields and their fallbacks.
3. In **Select Recipients**, choose **Email List** or **Segment**, then select the audience. If needed, choose an exclusion segment. Review the resulting count.
4. In **Schedule**, add up to three test email addresses and send a test. Open it on a desktop and phone; check subject, sender, layout, links, and personalization.
5. Choose immediate sending or a future date/time and its timezone. The timezone you select determines when the scheduled send runs.
6. In **Review & Send**, check each section again. Use the edit controls to correct anything before selecting **Send Campaign Now** or **Schedule Campaign**.

Use **Save Draft** when the campaign is not ready. A saved draft has not been sent.

<Warning>
Sending starts delivery to your audience. Check the audience and exclusions before confirming; messages already delivered cannot be recalled.
</Warning>

## Confirm the result

Return to the campaign list and check the status. A scheduled campaign should show **Scheduled**; an immediate campaign progresses through **Sending** to **Sent**. Large audiences can take time because delivery is processed in batches.

**Sent** describes campaign processing, not a guarantee that every recipient received the message. Use [analytics](/email/analytics) to examine deliveries, bounces, opens, and clicks.

The audience count is a preview. Unsubscribes and other suppression checks are applied during sending, so the final sent count can be smaller. Re-adding someone to a list does not guarantee that a suppressed address can receive email.

## Edit, duplicate, or cancel

Use the campaign list's available actions to edit eligible drafts/scheduled campaigns, duplicate a campaign into a new draft, or cancel a schedule. Confirm the resulting status after the action. If a campaign is already sending, cancellation cannot undo messages already sent.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| No audience is available | Create a list or segment and confirm you are using the intended sending-domain context. |
| Recipient count is zero or lower than expected | Check list membership, segment conditions, exclusions, consent, and suppressed addresses. |
| Sender/domain error | Use an active verified domain and a From address belonging to it. |
| Unsubscribe error | Add the unsubscribe merge tag using the editor; do not replace it with a plain-text promise to unsubscribe. |
| Test fails | Confirm the test addresses, saved message content, sender, and domain. Resolve the failure before scheduling. |
| A scheduled send is late | Check the selected timezone and campaign status. Capture the campaign name and scheduled time for **Get support**. |
| Sending or failed status persists | Use **Get support** with the campaign name, time, and visible error. Check delivery activity before starting a duplicate send. |

See [Templates](/email/templates) for message design and [Email troubleshooting](/email/troubleshooting) for recipient delivery problems.
